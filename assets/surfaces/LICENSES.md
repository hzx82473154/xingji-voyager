# Observed surface assets: provenance and reuse

Snapshot: 2026-10-09. 79 catalog bodies: 30 existing bodies and 49 additional bodies. Imported assets comprise 25 mission-derived maps, 7 observed shape meshes and 3 original mathematical schematic meshes and 45 individual photograph files; 70 records have an imported image. The other nine use existing native textures. This inventory does not claim that every known Solar System object has resolved surface data.

## Coverage and processing

- Photographs stay in the information card. A partial photograph is never wrapped around a globe as a supposed global map.
- Mission map gaps remain unfilled. Uranian moons, Triton, Pluto, Charon and other bodies have genuinely uneven or incomplete coverage. No AI imagery, upscaling or fabricated landforms were added.
- Phobos and Deimos retain the original NASA GLB geometry and exact matching UV atlases. Other observed models are simplified to at most 6,000 faces and centered/normalized; inferred unseen surfaces remain part of the original scientific model. Display attitude and spherical map alignment are illustrative, not a real-time spin-axis solution.
- Styx, Kerberos, Nereid, Braille and several encounter images contain inherently limited detail. Cropping removes neighboring objects and labels without creating new detail. All crop coordinates, source sizes, derived sizes and SHA-256 hashes are recorded in sources.json and photo-sources.json.
- Venus uses the Magellan-derived radar map from NASA 3D Resources. Radar brightness is not natural-color photography. Titan retains its atmospheric appearance; its supplementary photo shows Cassini/VIMS infrared terrain.
- Missing physical values stay absent. JPL orbital elements use their original reference plane and epoch; simplified propagation is not a precise ephemeris. Lucy mission measurements supersede the older Dinkinesh rotation estimate and Donaldjohanson unresolved size estimate.

## Reuse conditions

NASA/JPL public mission releases retain full mission and partner credits. JPL explicitly permits personal, journalistic and educational use of its credited partner images; NASA-owned images follow NASA media policy. Neither policy is an endorsement or a blanket third-party commercial grant. This edition is for personal noncommercial information viewing.

67P photograph retains CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO; its scientific shape dataset uses ESA science-archive CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO. The original ESA archive mesh bytes were checked against the processed-source SHA-256 and match exactly. Itokawa and Lutetia are explicitly released through ESO under CC BY 4.0, including the full JAXA/OSIRIS credits. Ryugu keeps ISAS/JAXA source-data CC BY4.0-compatible conditions and the image processing contributors' CC BY-SA4.0 conditions. Steins is rendered from an actual calibrated OSIRIS PDS observation under ESA's explicit 2018 all-OSIRIS CC BY-SA4.0 release, including asteroid flybys. Halley is a NASA Kuiper Airborne Observatory coma/tail photograph, not a resolved nucleus photo. No ESA Standard Licence press photos remain active.

Three unclear PDS geometry releases (Lutetia, Steins and Arrokoth) are removed from current distribution. Their replacements are original mathematical schematic meshes, not reconstructed observed terrain; known overall dimensions remain. The other seven observed meshes retain their respective explicit releases. No logo, human likeness or third-party press photograph is used as a mission data substitute.
